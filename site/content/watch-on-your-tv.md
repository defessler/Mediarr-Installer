This page is written for the person **receiving** the invite, not the person running
the server. If a friend or a family member has shared their Plex library with you,
this walks you through the whole thing, from the email to pressing play.

It assumes you've never set anything like this up before. Nothing here needs a
computer, and nothing here needs you to understand what a server is. Set aside about
fifteen minutes, and have your phone next to you for one step in the middle.

Note: If you're the one who runs the server and you came here to work out how to
invite somebody, skip to [For the Person Who Sent the Invite](#for-the-person-who-sent-the-invite).

## What You Need

- A TV, or a streaming stick or box plugged into one.
- The invite email.
- Your phone or a computer, just for one sign-in step.
- The Wi-Fi your TV is already using. There's nothing new to set up there.

You won't need to install anything on a computer, and the only thing you'll type on
the TV is a four-character code.

## Will This Cost Anything?

Maybe, and it's worth settling before you spend time on it.

Plex used to let you watch someone else's library over the internet for free. That
changed in 2025. At time of writing, one of these two things has to be true:

- The server owner has a Plex Pass - everyone they share with can watch for free, including you. There's nothing for you to buy and nothing for you to do.
- You have a Remote Watch Pass - about $2 a month or $20 a year, bought on your own Plex account.

Watching without either of those is only free when you're in the same house as the
server, on the same Wi-Fi. If the library belongs to a friend across town, you count
as "remote" every time you press play, even sitting on your own sofa.

So the easy move is to ask whoever invited you whether they have a Plex Pass. If they
do, you can stop thinking about it. If they don't, one Plex Pass on their account
covers everybody they've shared with at once, which usually works out cheaper than
each of you buying a Remote Watch Pass separately.

Note: Plex has been switching this on one platform at a time through 2025 and 2026,
starting with Roku. An app that lets you watch today might start asking next month.
The answer is the same either way.

## Step 1: Accept the Invite

1. **Find the email.** It comes from Plex and says someone wants to share their library with you. Check your spam or junk folder if it isn't in your inbox, because it lands there more often than it should.
2. **Click the button in the email** that accepts the invitation.
3. **Sign in, or make an account.** If you've never used Plex before, this is where you create one. It's free. You can use "Continue with Google" or "Continue with Apple" if you'd rather not invent another password.
4. That's the invite accepted. You should be able to see their library right away in the web page that opens.

Note: If the email never turns up at all, you can accept without it. Go to
`app.plex.tv` in any web browser, sign in, then open Settings -> Manage Library
Access and look under "Library Invitations Received". Accept it with the checkmark.

## Step 2: Install Plex on Your TV

Find your TV in the list below. If you're not sure what you've got, the brand name is
usually printed on the front of the TV, and the remote is a giveaway too.

- [Samsung](#samsung)
- [LG](#lg)
- [Roku Sticks and Roku TVs](#roku-sticks-and-roku-tvs) (TCL, Hisense, Sharp, Philips, onn)
- [Amazon Fire TV](#amazon-fire-tv) (Toshiba, Insignia, some Hisense)
- [Google TV and Android TV](#google-tv-and-android-tv) (Sony, TCL, Hisense, Philips, Nvidia Shield)
- [Vizio](#vizio)
- [Hisense VIDAA](#hisense-vidaa)
- [Apple TV](#apple-tv)
- [Xbox and PlayStation](#xbox-and-playstation)
- [None of These Match Your TV](#none-of-these-match-your-tv)

### Samsung

Samsung's own TVs from 2016 onward have a Plex app.

1. Press the **Home** button on the remote (the little house icon).
2. Find **"Apps"** in the row along the bottom of the screen.
3. Pick the **magnifying glass** at the top and type `Plex`.
4. Choose Plex, select **"Install"**, then **"Open"**.

Note: If the search turns up nothing, your TV is most likely from 2015 or earlier,
and Plex doesn't build an app for those any more. A streaming stick fixes it, see
[None of These Match Your TV](#none-of-these-match-your-tv).

### LG

LG TVs run webOS. 4K models from 2016 onward should be able to run the app.

1. Press the **Home** button (the house icon). If you have the pointer remote, aka the Magic Remote, it's the button in the middle.
2. Open the **"LG Content Store"**. Newer sets just call it **"Apps"**.
3. Search for `Plex`.
4. Select **"Install"**, then **"Launch"**.

### Roku Sticks and Roku TVs

This covers the Roku Express and Streaming Stick, plus every TV with Roku built in.
TCL, Hisense, Sharp, Philips, and onn all make them.

1. Press **Home** (the house icon) on the purple Roku remote.
2. Scroll down the menu on the left to **"Streaming Channels"**. Some versions call it **"Channel Store"**.
3. Choose **"Search Channels"** and type `Plex`.
4. Select Plex, then **"Add Channel"**. It lands at the bottom of your channel list on the home screen.

Note: Roku was the first platform Plex switched the remote-watching rules on, so this
is the one most likely to ask you about a pass. See [Will This Cost Anything?](#will-this-cost-anything)

### Amazon Fire TV

This covers the Fire TV Stick and Cube, plus TVs with Fire TV built in. Toshiba,
Insignia, some Hisense and Pioneer sets, and Amazon's own TVs all use it.

1. From the home screen, pick the **magnifying glass** at the top left. Or hold the microphone button on the remote and just say "Plex".
2. Type or say `Plex`.
3. Choose the Plex app, then **"Get"** or **"Download"**.
4. Choose **"Open"**.

### Google TV and Android TV

This is the biggest group. Most Sony, TCL, Hisense, Philips, and Sharp TVs sold in the
last few years run one of these, and so do the Google TV Streamer, the Chromecast with
Google TV, the Nvidia Shield, and a lot of unbranded boxes.

1. Press **Home**.
2. Go to the **"Apps"** tab along the top, or open the **Google Play Store**.
3. Use the **magnifying glass** to search, and type or speak `Plex`.
4. Select **"Install"**, then **"Open"**.

Tip: Typing on a TV is miserable. If you have an Android phone signed in to the same
Google account, you can open the Play Store on the phone, search for Plex, and pick
your TV from the install menu. It appears on the TV a minute later.

### Vizio

Vizio TVs run SmartCast, which comes with its apps already on the TV rather than in a
store you browse.

1. Press the **Home** button. On older remotes it's the **"V"** button.
2. Look along the row of apps for **Plex** and open it. There's usually nothing to install.
3. On sets from around 2018 onward, if Plex isn't in the row, you should be able to open the app store on the TV and search for it.

Note: Vizio sets older than roughly 2016 have no apps at all and can only be cast to
from a phone. Casting does work, but your phone has to stay awake and involved the
whole time, so a cheap stick makes a much better daily experience.

### Hisense VIDAA

Hisense is confusing, because they ship two different systems. Some of their TVs run
Google TV, in which case use the [Google TV and Android TV](#google-tv-and-android-tv)
steps above. The rest run Hisense's own system, called VIDAA.

VIDAA is the one platform on this page where Plex isn't reliably available, and it
varies by country and by model.

1. Press **Home**, open the app store on the TV, and search for `Plex`.
2. If it's there, install it and carry on to the next step.
3. If it isn't, don't fight it. Get a streaming stick, see [None of These Match Your TV](#none-of-these-match-your-tv).

### Apple TV

This means the little black box that plugs into your TV, not the Apple TV app.

1. Open the **"App Store"** on the Apple TV home screen.
2. Search for `Plex`.
3. Select **"Get"**.

Note: Plenty of smart TVs have an app called "Apple TV" built into them. That's a
different thing, and Plex won't be inside it.

### Xbox and PlayStation

Both have a proper Plex app. If a console is already plugged into the TV, that saves
you buying anything at all.

- Xbox One, Series S, and Series X - open the **"Microsoft Store"**, search for Plex, and install it.
- PlayStation 4 and 5 - open the **"PlayStation Store"**, search for Plex, and download it.

### None of These Match Your TV

Any TV with an HDMI socket can run Plex, because you can plug the apps in. A streaming
stick is a small dongle that goes into a spare HDMI socket on the back of the TV. It
brings its own remote, its own home screen, and its own apps, and your TV just becomes
a screen. This is also the answer for a TV that's simply too old.

Good options at time of writing, all around $30 to $40:

- Roku Express or Roku Streaming Stick - the simplest remote and the plainest menus, which makes it the easiest one if you're not confident with tech.
- Amazon Fire TV Stick - cheap and dependable, though the home screen pushes Amazon's own content at you.
- Google TV Streamer - the nicest of the three if you already use Google or Android things.

Plug it into a spare HDMI socket, plug in its power lead, then press the **"Source"**
or **"Input"** button on your TV remote until you see its home screen. From there,
follow the matching section above.

## Step 3: Sign In on the TV

This is the part people expect to be painful. It isn't, because you never type your
password on the TV.

1. Open the **Plex** app on the TV.
2. Choose **"Sign In"**.
3. The TV shows a **four-character code**, something like `H4KP`. Leave it on the screen.
4. On your phone or computer, open a web browser and go to `plex.tv/link`.
5. Sign in with the Plex account from Step 1, if it asks you to.
6. Type in the four characters from the TV and submit them.
7. Within a few seconds the TV signs itself in. You don't have to press anything on it.

Note: Those codes expire after a few minutes. If the site says the code is wrong or
expired, go back to the TV, come out of the sign-in screen and into it again, and use
the fresh code it gives you.

## Step 4: Find Their Library

This is the step that trips most people up. When Plex opens, the home screen is mostly
Plex's own free movies and channels, which isn't what you're here for. Your friend's
library lives somewhere else in the app.

Plex has been redesigning its TV apps through 2026, so you'll see one of two layouts.

**The newer layout** puts a row of tabs along the bottom or the side, one of which is
**"Libraries"**. Open that, and their libraries are listed under the name of their
server, something like "Doug's Media Server". Movies, TV Shows, and Music are usually
separate entries under it.

**The older layout** has a menu that slides out from the side. Press left on the
remote to open it, or look for the "hamburger" button, which is three stacked lines.
Scroll past your own things to the bottom, and their server name is sitting there with
its libraries underneath.

Either way, once you've found it: open a library, pick something, and press play.
That's the whole job done.

Tip: Most versions of the app let you pin or favourite a library so it turns up on the
home screen next time. It's worth doing on the first evening, because it saves you the
hunt every time after that.

## If Something Goes Wrong

Almost everything that goes wrong here is one of six things.

- The invite email never arrived - check spam and junk first. Failing that, sign in at `app.plex.tv` and accept it under Settings -> Manage Library Access -> "Library Invitations Received".
- It asks you to subscribe before it will play - that's the remote-watching rule, see [Will This Cost Anything?](#will-this-cost-anything). Ask whoever invited you whether they have a Plex Pass.
- The four-character code won't work - it expired. Back out of the sign-in screen on the TV, go in again, and use the new code.
- You can see free Plex movies but not their library - you're still on the home screen rather than in the libraries, see [Step 4: Find Their Library](#step-4-find-their-library).
- It buffers, or the picture keeps going soft - this comes down to the owner's upload speed and your own connection, and the app can't fix either. There's a video quality setting in the app's settings. Turning it down from "Original" to something like 4 Mbps usually smooths playback out.
- Nothing plays and the server looks offline - their machine is switched off, or their internet is down. There's nothing you can do at your end, so message them.

If none of that helps, tell whoever invited you exactly what the TV says on screen,
word for word. They can see far more from their end than you can from yours.

## If They Run Jellyfin Instead

Some people run Jellyfin rather than Plex. It does the same job and it's completely
free. It works a little differently, since there are no invite emails and no Plex
account involved. Instead they'll send you three things: a web address, a username,
and a password.

Install **"Jellyfin"** from the same app store as above. It's on Roku, Fire TV,
Android TV and Google TV, Apple TV, LG, and Samsung. Open it, type in the address they
gave you, then sign in with the username and password. After that it behaves much like
everything described above.

## For the Person Who Sent the Invite

The other half of this page, for whoever runs the server.

To invite somebody:

1. Go to `app.plex.tv` and sign in.
2. Open Settings -> Manage Library Access.
3. Choose to invite a friend, and put in their email address or their Plex username.
4. Tick the libraries you want them to have. You don't have to share all of them.
5. Send it, then send them a link to this page.

Two things worth knowing before you do:

- Your Plex Pass covers everyone you share with - if you have one, none of your friends need to buy anything to watch from their own homes. If you don't, each of them needs their own Remote Watch Pass. One Plex Pass is usually both the cheaper and the kinder answer.
- Let them request things as well - Seerr is the request portal we wire up as part of the stack, and it's a far better experience than being texted a list of titles. Check out [Requesting Movies & TV](requesting) for how to add people to it.
